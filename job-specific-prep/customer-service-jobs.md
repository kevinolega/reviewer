## **1. Understand the Role**

Customer service reps handle customer concerns, questions, and complaints.  
They solve problems and keep customers satisfied.

### Core Tasks:
- Answer calls, emails, or chats  
- Help customers with orders, billing, or account issues  
- Handle complaints calmly  
- Follow scripts and procedures  
- Record interactions using tools (CRM, ticketing system)

---

## **2. Learn the Common Tools**

Familiarity with tools helps during interviews.

### Examples:
- **Zendesk** – ticketing system  
- **Freshdesk** – support dashboard  
- **Salesforce** – CRM tool  
- **Google Workspace** – email, calendar, docs  
- **MS Excel or Google Sheets** – for logging cases

---

## **3. Practice Customer Service Scenarios**

### Common Mock Call Scenarios:
- Billing error  
- Order not received  
- Product arrived damaged  
- Login issues  
- Requesting a refund

### Sample Spiel Structure:
- “Thank you for calling [Company]. My name is [Name]. How can I help you today?”  
- “I understand how that can be frustrating.”  
- “Let me check that for you. Please give me a moment.”  
- “Here’s what I can do to fix this…”  
- “Is there anything else I can assist you with?”

## **Prepare Interview Answers Using Stories**

### Format 1: **Hero’s Journey**
Use this when sharing a **success story**.

1. **Goal** – What were you trying to achieve?  
2. **Journey** – What steps did you take?  
3. **Struggles** – What problems did you face?  
4. **Success** – What result did you achieve?

**Example:**  
> “My goal was to improve our call resolution rate. We had many repeat callers, and our QA scores were dropping.  
> I started tracking common issues and shared a fix-it guide with my team.  
> At first, it was hard to get everyone to use it consistently, but I kept updating it and asked feedback from agents.  
> Within two months, our FCR rate improved by 15%, and our QA scores went up.”

---

### Format 2: **Hero’s Tragedy**
Use this when sharing a **failure story** and what you learned.

1. **Goal** – What were you trying to do?  
2. **Journey** – What did you try?  
3. **Struggles** – What went wrong?  
4. **Lesson** – What did you learn or change?

**Example:**  
> “I wanted to handle more tickets to prove I could take on a leadership role.  
> I rushed through cases to hit high numbers.  
> I ended up missing key details, and QA flagged my work.  
> I realized I was chasing numbers and not quality.  
> Since then, I focused on doing each task right the first time. My accuracy improved, and I still hit my targets — now with better customer feedback.”

## **6. Practice Phone Etiquette**

- Speak clearly and slowly  
- Use a friendly, respectful tone  
- Avoid filler words like “uh” and “um”  
- Smile while talking – it changes your voice  
- Use short, complete sentences

---

## **7. Improve Communication Skills**

- Read customer service scripts aloud  
- Record your voice, then review  
- Watch call center videos on YouTube  
- Shadow or copy native English speakers using movies or podcasts

---

## **8. Bonus: Learn Common Metrics (if asked)**

- **AHT (Average Handle Time)** – How long you take per call  
- **FCR (First Call Resolution)** – Fixing the issue on the first call  
- **CSAT (Customer Satisfaction Score)** – Rating given by customers  
- **QA (Quality Assurance)** – How well you followed company standards
