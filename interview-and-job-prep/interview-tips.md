### Preparation

- **Study the Company**  
  - Know what they do, who they help, and how they make money.  
  - Visit their website, social media, and job post.

- **Understand the Role**  
  - Review the job duties.  
  - Match your experience with the tasks they listed.

- **Practice Common Questions**  
  - “Tell me about yourself.”  
  - “Why should we hire you?”  
  - “What are your strengths and weaknesses?”  
  - “Why do you want to work here?”

- **Prepare Questions to Ask Them**  
  - “What does a normal day look like?”  
  - “What does success look like in this role?”

---

### Presentation

- **Dress Properly**  
  - Match the company’s dress code.  
  - If unsure, go clean and professional.

- **Be on Time**  
  - Arrive 10–15 minutes early.  
  - For online interviews, test your camera, mic, and internet before the call.

- **Speak Clearly and Calmly**  
  - Use short sentences.  
  - Don’t speak too fast.  

- **Control Nervous Habits**  
  - Avoid saying “uh,” “um,” or fidgeting.  
  - Breathe before answering.

---

### Communication

- **Answer the Question**  
  - Don’t go off-topic.  
  - Use examples from past jobs to prove your point.

## **Prepare Interview Answers Using Stories**

### Format 1: **Hero’s Journey**
Use this when sharing a **success story**.

1. **Goal** – What were you trying to achieve?  
2. **Journey** – What steps did you take?  
3. **Struggles** – What problems did you face?  
4. **Success** – What result did you achieve?

**Example:**  
> “My goal was to improve our call resolution rate. We had many repeat callers, and our QA scores were dropping.  
> I started tracking common issues and shared a fix-it guide with my team.  
> At first, it was hard to get everyone to use it consistently, but I kept updating it and asked feedback from agents.  
> Within two months, our FCR rate improved by 15%, and our QA scores went up.”

---

### Format 2: **Hero’s Tragedy**
Use this when sharing a **failure story** and what you learned.

1. **Goal** – What were you trying to do?  
2. **Journey** – What did you try?  
3. **Struggles** – What went wrong?  
4. **Lesson** – What did you learn or change?

**Example:**  
> “I wanted to handle more tickets to prove I could take on a leadership role.  
> I rushed through cases to hit high numbers.  
> I ended up missing key details, and QA flagged my work.  
> I realized I was chasing numbers and not quality.  
> Since then, I focused on doing each task right the first time. My accuracy improved, and I still hit my targets — now with better customer feedback.”

---

- **Be Honest About Weaknesses**  
  - Share real challenges, but explain how you’re improving.  
  - Don’t say “I work too hard” — that sounds fake.

- **Show Interest and Positivity**  
  - Don’t complain about old jobs or bosses.  
  - Focus on what you learned and what you want to do next.

---

### After the Interview

- **Thank the Interviewer**  
  - Say thank you before you leave or after the call ends.  
  - Optional: Send a short thank-you message.

- **Reflect Right Away**  
  - Write down what you remember: good points, weak points, questions they asked.  
  - Use it to prepare better for the next one.
